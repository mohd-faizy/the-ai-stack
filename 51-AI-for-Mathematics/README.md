# 51 - AI for Mathematics

> Formal verification, interactive theorem proving, automated reasoning, and foundation models advancing mathematical discovery and formal proof synthesis.

## 🌐 Overview & Architectural Importance

Large language models are notoriously prone to calculation errors. Formal mathematics pairs generative AI with verified proof assistants (like Lean 4 and Isabelle), guaranteeing that every step in a multi-page proof is mathematically sound with zero logical hallucinations.

## 📑 Curated Learning Guides

Explore in-depth roadmaps, authoritative textbooks, official documentation, courses, and implementations for each subtopic:

* [**Theorem Proving**](Theorem-Proving.md) — Lean 4, Mathlib, Isabelle/HOL, Coq, formal proof checkers, and interactive theorem proving.
* [**Mathematical Reasoning**](Mathematical-Reasoning.md) — MiniF2F, LeanDojo, DeepSeek-Prover, automated premise selection, and neural guidance.
* [**Computational Mathematics**](Computational-Mathematics.md) — SageMath, Julia, SymPy, computer algebra systems, and symbolic computation.

## 🧭 Recommended Study Sequence

1. **Formal Verification Fundamentals**: Interactive proof assistants (Lean 4, Isabelle) and calculus of constructions.
2. **The Mathlib Ecosystem**: The collaborative formalization of modern undergraduate and research mathematics.
3. **Neural Theorem Proving**: Interfacing LLMs with formal proof environments using LeanDojo.
4. **Synthetic Proof Search**: Monte Carlo Tree Search (MCTS) and reinforcement learning over tactic steps.
5. **Symbolic Computation**: Integrating computer algebra systems (SymPy, SageMath) with numerical pipelines.

## 🗝️ Foundational Prerequisites

- Discrete mathematics, mathematical logic, and proof writing (induction, contradiction).
- Type theory fundamentals (Curry-Howard correspondence: proofs as programs).
- Functional programming in Lean 4 or Coq.
